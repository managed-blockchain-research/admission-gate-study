Only block where the per-sender bucket treats the classes differently, and it does so only because they were given different sender rates.
- X3 (attack 12 senders / look-alike 6 senders, all about 0.55 tx/s): SenderBucket admits all heavy traffic (1,206 of 1,206), attack block 0.000, gain +64 [-317, 445] (no effect); the global bucket (BudgetR250: attack block 0.833, receipts 20,529) and SizeStatic do act. Fragmentation of the same total over more accounts bypasses the per-sender bucket.
- X4 (attack 12 senders / look-alike 1 sender at 3.33 tx/s): inverted: look-alike rejected 277 of 400 in every seed, attack block 0.000; gain +1,054 [1,016, 1,091].
## Allowed wording
"Under this fixed workload and with the sender account as identity, a per-sender heavy-byte bucket admitted senders below its rate and throttled senders above it; its outcome for look-alike versus attack followed how the load was partitioned over accounts (look-alike untouched in X2, nothing throttled in X3, look-alike throttled and attack passed in X4), as expected before the data.
