## 11fe. RAAC journal-resubmission evidence track (IEEE Access, Access-2026-49279): offline re-analysis of stored runs, one service load test, Slurm QOS gate; registered 2026-11-18

**1. Decision (owner, 2026-11-18; agy and codex opinions merged by the coordinator).** The RAAC manuscript received reject-with-resubmission (one resubmission allowed). The resubmission will be rewritten around the registered campaign data only (no pre-audit pilot figures). Working thesis, subject to the data: the effect of pressure-based admission depends on runtime, heap and collector; blanket pressure shedding (PressureOnly) trades benign-traffic rejection and throughput for GC reduction; selective blocking is possible only where attack and benign traffic are separable in the feature space (the registered detector result of 11y/11q: ref_infeasible, rollup-like look-alike scores above every attack, hard look-alike identical on the wire). No claim that the learned score beats rules is made or implied.

**2. Registered work (all effect-blind to treatment choice; stored run directories are read-only inputs).**
(a) Offline analyses written to `~/work/analysis/20261118_access_resub/`: per-class confirmation-latency quantiles (p50/p95/p99/p99.9) from `tierstats/w*.json` (sent-time, latency, flag), reported together with rejection and failure counts because latency exists only for confirmed transactions and is quantized by the Caliper polling interval; block-interval and stall statistics from `chain.csv`; one joint table per client and cell of GC pause, on-chain transactions, benign rejection rate during the attack window and latency; paired same-seed contrasts none versus PressureOnly with t and bootstrap intervals and effect sizes (the 11fc NM 2048 MiB pairs are exploratory, per-seed values only); a detector-condition analysis scoring the stored per-transaction table (`diag/all_scored.csv.gz`, sha256 a720ddfc...) with the frozen IsolationForest, RuleSize and Oracle score, reporting ROC/operating points with and without the look-alike classes. Raw per-run values are published with every table.
(b) One harness measurement, no Besu/Nethermind: a load test of the frozen `~/diss_impl/raac/serve_unified.py` (read-only copy, sha256 recorded at run start) at 100, 300, 1000 and 3000 requests/s: decision latency p50/p99/p99.9, service CPU and RSS, saturation point, and behavior with the service stopped, slowed (injected delay above the client timeout) and flooded (fail-open versus fail-closed as implemented). Non-exclusive, one node, `--qos=short`, at most 2 h, output under `~/work/svc_bench/`, raw logs deleted after parsing per the cleanup rule. Exploratory: descriptive values with run-to-run spread, no p-values.

**3. Not registered here (conditional).** RAAC under ZGC, a PressureOnly operating-point sweep and a hysteresis or decay controller are candidates only; each needs its own addendum after (a) shows whether it is needed. No new Besu/Nethermind run is authorized by this addendum.

**4. Operational change.** `sbatch_gated.sh` now adds `--qos=short` (limit <= 2:00:00) or `--qos=long` when the caller gives no `--qos`/`-q` (cluster notice 2026-11-18, mandatory from 2026-11-23); backup `sbatch_gated.sh.pre_qos`; five dry runs checked. No design, seed, node-hour or analysis change; frozen launchers, manifest and validator untouched.

**5. Not claimed.** Nothing confirmatory beyond what earlier addenda registered; no cross-client pooling; no mainnet-traffic claim; no latency improvement is asserted before the analysis in (a) is read.


## 11ff. Besu 512 MB matched-volume admission experiment and ZGC replication (exploratory; IEEE Access resubmission); registered 2026-11-18

**1. Why (owner approval 2026-11-18).** Stage 1 (stored, immutable) on Besu 512 MB with G1 gives total GC pause none 23,392 ms, PressureOnly 15,413 ms (benign rejection 13.8 percent, benign confirmation latency p50 18.0 s -> 7.5 s, six same-seed pairs), but OracleScore 23,823 ms, RuleSize 23,024 ms, LearnedScore 24,889 ms. Oracle/RuleSize reject only about 217 transactions per run (pressure-graduated, flagged only) against 2,814 for PressureOnly, so selectivity and shed volume are confounded. Reviewers ask for a matched comparison (R1-10, R1-12, R2-7) and RAAC under ZGC (R1-13, R2-9).

**2. Design.** Besu 25.1.0 r6 build, 512 MB, workload v5 raac2 profile unchanged (100 tx/s, 240 s, attack burst 60-180 s, 36 KB, G = 125,000,000), node GOV_MODE=OFF in every arm, node cpu04 non-exclusive, one job at a time. G1, seeds 61001-61004: arms none, PressureOnly (ramp 0.60-0.90, the Stage 1 arm), OracleBlock (label-flagged transactions rejected with p = 1, benign never; AI_GRAD_LOW=-1, AI_GRAD_HIGH=-0.5 on serve_oracle.py), PressureOnlyLow (ramp 0.50-0.80), PressureOnlyHigh (ramp 0.70-0.95): 20 runs. Generational ZGC (-XX:+UseZGC -XX:+ZGenerational), seeds 61101-61104: arms none, PressureOnly, OracleBlock: 12 runs. Per-seed arm order `Random(seed).shuffle(arms)` (plan file `acc/ACC_PLAN.json`). One smoke run per mode family first (seed 61099, SMOKE=1, ZGC none and G1 OracleBlock), not counted; the main chain starts only after the smoke runs show a summary and a working u_t channel. Re-run only infrastructure-invalid runs, once per seed; the chain stops on a refused submission.
Reported per arm: total and p99 GC pause (ZGC: allocation stalls and concurrent cycle counts in addition), benign confirmation latency p50/p95 and confirmed benign count (workers 0-26 and class S/M/L from tierstats), benign and attack rejection counts (service counters), on-chain transactions, OOM/crash, peak heap, node load. Paired same-seed differences against none with t 95 percent intervals and per-pair values; exploratory, no p-values, no pooling of G1 and ZGC.
**Pre-stated readings.** If OracleBlock matches PressureOnly on latency and GC at zero benign rejection, selective blocking with a separable signal dominates blanket shedding on this workload; if OracleBlock does not, shed volume (backlog relief) rather than attack removal explains the Besu effect; the ramp variants show how much of the PressureOnly effect scales with rejection volume. Both outcomes are reported.

**3. Apparatus (new files; no frozen file is edited).** `~/work/acc/acc_raac_run.sbatch` is a copy of the frozen `run_raac_11az.sbatch` (sha256 93c0bd2613a0...) with one anchored block (arm variants map PressureOnlyLow/High and OracleBlock onto the registered base arms PressureOnly/OracleScore and append a per-variant service environment after $SENV) and one added line in raac_assert.txt; `acc_raac_checked.sbatch` + `acc_selfcheck.py` replace the validator essentials at job start (hardware class cpu01-05, manifest pins of the frozen service/detector/workload files, r6 Besu jar sha256, JDK 21.0.12, exports, token grammar, arm and seed ranges, single-use run ids; refusals are logged); these files are outside the submit_validator.py registry by name and are pinned in `acc/ACC_PINS.json` (sha256 prefixes: launcher 320a977dffeb, self-check 2e5443803d19, wrapper ed87b3ddc756, Besu main jar 2438c9a018b6). Submission only through `sbatch_gated.sh` (patched in 11fe for --qos and local Slurm). The stored run directories, manifest and validator are unchanged.

**4. Cost and limits.** 32 runs, about 9 minutes each, sequential: about 5 node-hours on one non-exclusive node, `--qos=short` per run. If a smoke run shows an apparatus error the chain stops and nothing counts.

**5. Not claimed.** Nothing for Nethermind; no confirmatory claim; no statement on pacing or low-rate attacks; the Besu 512 MB heap is the only stress cell here.


## 11gg. Besu 1 GB replication of the 11ff matched-selectivity experiment (ZGC primary, G1 secondary); exploratory; registered 2026-11-18

**1. Why (owner approval 2026-11-18).** 11ff (stored, 32 runs, Besu 512 MB) showed: G1 - label-based selective blocking (OracleBlock) gave the largest benign confirmation gain (+1,367 vs PressureOnly, 4/4 same-seed pairs; PressureOnly better on GC pause and confirmed-only p50 latency); ZGC - only PressureOnly finished without OOM (4/4), at about 10,600 rejected benign transactions per run, while none and OracleBlock crashed 4/4. Both results are at ONE heap size and the collector was the only factor changed, so no statement about heap slack is supported. 11gg adds the same arms at 1 GB to see how survival and benign confirmation change with heap for each collector.

**2. Design.** Identical to 11ff except heap 1 GB (token `besu:1:100:<mode>:OFF:36:125000000:<seed>:C:new:`, heap_mb 1024, -Xmx=-Xms): launcher `acc_raac_run.sbatch` unchanged (sha256 320a977dffeb), r6 Besu build, workload v5 raac2 profile (100 tx/s, 240 s, burst 60-180 s, 36 KB, G = 125,000,000), GOV_MODE=OFF, node cpu04 non-exclusive, one job at a time. Arms none, PressureOnly (ramp 0.60-0.90), OracleBlock. Generational ZGC (primary), seeds 61201-61204, 12 runs; G1 (secondary, so that collector and heap effects are not confounded), seeds 61301-61304, 12 runs; ZGC first. Per-seed arm order `Random(seed).shuffle(arms)` (plan `acc/ACC_PLAN_11gg.json`). One smoke run (ZGC none 1 GB, seed 61299, SMOKE=1, not counted) must show a summary with heap_mb 1024 and mode zgc, otherwise the chain stops.
Endpoints and analysis as in 11ff: survival (OOM / node death), time to node death, benign confirmation in the burst window (workers 0-26, classes S/M/L), benign and attack rejection counts, GC pause (G1) / allocation stall evidence (ZGC), on-chain over offered; same-seed paired differences with t 95 percent intervals and per-pair values, direct contrasts OracleBlock vs PressureOnly and each arm vs none; exploratory, no p-values, no pooling across collectors or heaps. Confirmed-only latency is reported with the rejection counts (survivorship).
**Pre-stated readings.** Any pattern is reported. No claim that heap size is the cause of a difference between collectors is made from 11ff alone; 11gg gives a within-collector heap contrast (512 MB vs 1 GB) only.

**3. Apparatus (new files; nothing frozen edited).** `acc_selfcheck_11gg.py` and `acc_raac_checked_11gg.sbatch` are copies of the 11ff self-check and wrapper with the heap token `1` and run-id prefix ACC11GG_ (sha256 prefixes: self-check 6f549c4245c3, wrapper 18db6ea5f9e1), pinned in `acc/ACC_PINS_11gg.json` together with the unchanged launcher; submit helper `ACC_SUBMIT_11gg.sh`, chain `acc_chain_11gg.sh`. ## 11hh. Nethermind 2048 MiB matched-selectivity experiment (none / PressureOnly / OracleBlock); exploratory; registered 2026-11-18

**1. Why (owner approval 2026-11-18).** Stored Nethermind RAAC data show no effect of PressureOnly: Stage 1 at 2.5 GiB (n = 6 pairs; pressure never reached the 0.60 ramp) and 11fc at 2048 MiB (5 pairs; u_t maximum 0.72-0.75, u_t >= 0.60 for about half of each run, PressureOnly rejected about 1,900 decisions per run, on-chain over offered about 0.945-0.948 in both arms, no node death). Arms none, PressureOnly (ramp 0.60-0.90), OracleBlock (label-flagged transactions rejected with p = 1). Seeds 62001-62005, 15 runs, per-seed arm order `Random(seed).shuffle(arms)` (plan `acc/ACC_PLAN_11hh.json`); one smoke run (none, seed 62099, SMOKE=1, not counted) must show a summary with heap_mb 2048 and a Nethermind kind, otherwise the chain stops. The chain starts only after the 11gg chain has ended (never two of my jobs at a time).
Endpoints: survival (OOM lines, node death), on-chain over offered, benign confirmation in the burst window and over the whole run (workers 0-26, classes S/M/L), benign and attack rejection counts by time window (before the burst [0,60) s, during [60,180) s, after [180,300) s), together with the u_t values and the u_t file update timestamps of the same windows (the service reads u_t = post-GC gen2+LOH occupancy over the ENFORCED limit from the governor trace, source ut_file, exported in every arm including OFF; it does NOT read RSS, so no explanation in terms of RSS persistence is assumed: any delay of rejections is reported from the u_t series itself), confirmed benign transactions and confirmed-only latency (p50, p95) in the burst window AND the recovery window [180,300) s, service counters (total, flagged, rejected_flagged, rejected_unflagged, decisions_with_p_gt_0, stale decisions, max reject probability), peak RSS, u_t statistics, GC pause only if the NettraceGcParser validation conditions of 11p hold (otherwise not reported). ## 11ii. Calldata-size admission baselines and controls at system level (Besu G1 512 MB, attack and look-alike cells); exploratory, post-hoc threshold; registered 2026-11-18 evening (owner delegated overnight execution)

**1. Why.** Reviewers asked for calibrated deployable baselines (calldata size) and whether pressure feedback matters (R2-7, R1-10/12). 11ff-11hh showed that a ground-truth filter that is independent of u_t (p = 1) gave the best benign receipt timeliness, whereas pressure-graded blanket shedding gave smaller or no gains and rejected benign requests; the learned scorer was inert and lost to size rules in offline detection analysis. 11ii tests at system level a deployable size rule applied statically versus pressure-graded, with two controls added after an independent design review (Codex, blind, 2026-11-18): a fixed-probability size control and a content-blind volume control.

**2. Design.** Besu 25.1.0 r6 build, G1, 512 MB (token `besu:512m:100:g1:OFF:36:125000000:<seed>:C:new:`), workload v5 raac2 profile unchanged (100 tx/s, 240 s, burst 60-180 s), node cpu05 (Xeon Gold 5317 class, idle at start; all runs on the same node), non-exclusive, one job at a time, launcher `acc_raac_run_11ii.sbatch` (copy of the 11ff candidate, sha256 8462f10fe514..., plus arm variants), seeds per cell and per-seed arm order `Random(seed).shuffle(arms)` (plan `acc/ACC_PLAN_11ii.json`).
Attack cell (burst attack, seeds 63001-63005, 35 runs): none; OracleBlock (ground-truth filter, p = 1, independent of u_t); PressureOnly (blanket shedding, ramp 0.60-0.90); SizeStatic (reject if calldata > 65,604 B, p = 1, independent of u_t; service mode rule_size, threshold 65,605, AI_GRAD_LOW=-1, AI_GRAD_HIGH=-0.5); SizeGraded (same rule with p = grad(u_t), ramp 0.60-0.90); SizeFixedP20 (same rule with constant p = 0.20: AI_GRAD_LOW=-200, AI_GRAD_HIGH=800 gives grad(u) = 0.200 to 0.201 for u in [0,1]; the value 0.20 is the share of flagged transactions rejected by the Stage-1 RuleSize arm, taken from stored earlier data); RandShed48 (content-blind volume control: every transaction rejected with p = 0.048-0.049, AI_GRAD_LOW=-48, AI_GRAD_HIGH=952, equal to the attack share 1,200/25,200).
Look-alike cell (burst lookalike, rollup profile: benign transactions with the attack's calldata size and opcode count, gas limit 2,151,000, no attack transactions; seeds 63101-63104, 20 runs): none; PressureOnly; SizeStatic; SizeGraded; SizeFixedP20. (OracleBlock equals none there; RandShed48 not run.)
Three smoke runs (seeds 63099/63199, SMOKE=1, not counted) must pass counter checks before the main chain: SizeStatic attack (flagged >= 1,100, all flagged rejected, no unflagged rejection), SizeFixedP20 look-alike (flagged >= 1,000, rejected share of flagged 0.10-0.30, no unflagged rejection), RandShed48 attack (rejected share of all decisions 0.025-0.075).

**3. Threshold disclosure.** 65,604 B is the largest calldata among the benign training classes of the detector (look-alike classes were excluded from training by the 11q design); it was chosen AFTER seeing the 11ff-11hh results and the offline detector analysis (the label-free fair rule of debate 023). Any size threshold at or above the look-alike size (98,372 B) would not flag the attack; so the threshold is a tuned baseline, not an independent one.

**4. Validity, stopping and exclusion rules (fixed before any 11ii data).** All scheduled runs are executed in the registered order; no run is replaced or repeated (no retry); a run without summary.json is reported as an apparatus failure and kept in the inventory; the chain stops if a smoke check fails, if a submission is refused, or after 3 consecutive runs without summary.json; a job exceeding 65 minutes is cancelled and counted as an apparatus failure. No post-hoc exclusion of completed runs. Outcome definitions as in METRIC_DEFINITIONS.md (receipt within the connector's 30 s limit; not sent; planned-without-record and unfinished reported separately; chain-scan counts not used as effects). Telemetry reported: service counters (total, flagged, rejected_flagged, rejected_unflagged, stale decisions, realized reject fraction), u_t statistics per window, ai.status (fail-open exposure), survival split. Analysis: same-seed paired differences with t 95 percent intervals over pair differences, per-pair values always shown, no p-values, no ranking by interval overlap, no pooling across cells.

**5. Interpretation limits (stated in advance).** SizeStatic versus SizeGraded differs in rejection intensity as well as in feedback; SizeFixedP20 is a fixed-probability control with a value from earlier data, not an intensity-matched control (the realized rejection of SizeGraded will differ); RandShed48 tests volume versus targeting only for the attack cell; attack and look-alike traffic are never in the same run, so nothing about mixed-traffic selectivity or deployability follows; one heap and collector; five (attack) and four (look-alike) seeds give imprecise intervals; execution-cost equality of the look-alike and the attack rests on the earlier class scan (same gasUsed) and is not re-verified here; the node is non-exclusive and other users' load is not controlled (idle cores and co-tenants are recorded per run).

**6. Apparatus and outputs.** New files only (launcher `acc_raac_run_11ii.sbatch` 8462f10fe514..., self-check `acc_selfcheck_11ii.py` 3dd455689bb2..., wrapper `acc_raac_checked_11ii.sbatch` e31885c9d8cf..., pins `ACC_PINS_11ii.json`, helper `ACC_SUBMIT_11ii.sh`, chain `acc_chain_11ii.sh`); frozen files and the validator are unchanged. At the end the chain writes `analysis/20261118_access_resub/MORNING_REPORT.md` (numbers only) and `acc/verify_11ii.out` (labels vs tierstats vs service counters). Cost: 55 runs plus 3 smoke, about 8.5 minutes each, about 8 node-hours non-exclusive, `--qos=short` per run.

**7. Not claimed.** Nothing about Nethermind, ZGC or other heaps; no causal attribution to pressure feedback; no deployability; no statement about benign transactions that the rule would reject beyond this workload.


## 11jj. Response-letter experiments: mixed attack and look-alike traffic, ZGC with the learned scorer, sustained and short-pulse attacks with a sticky (hysteresis) controller, Nethermind with a size filter; exploratory; registered 2026-11-19 (owner: run all experiments needed to answer the reviewers; Slurm submissions delegated)

**1. Why.** After 11ff-11ii the open reviewer items needing new evidence are: selectivity when attack and look-alike benign traffic are present together (R2-10, R1-10/12), the learned scorer under ZGC (R1-13), Nethermind provisioning evidence (R2-9), sustained and low-rate attacks and a hysteresis controller (R1-14, R2-4, R2-10). 11jj gives one block per item. Design reviewed blind by Codex on 2026-11-19 (adopted: content-blind volume control in the mixed block, provisioning/assertion reporting for Z and N, per-class and per-stage reporting, restricted claims, priority order).

**2. Common design.** Workload v5 raac2 (100 tx/s, 240 s, 30 workers, 36 KB), launcher `acc_raac_run_11jj.sbatch` (copy of the 11ii launcher, sha256 c3df1f8f72b4..., with arm variant SizeSticky, a service-directory override and mixed-burst module copies), GOV_MODE=OFF, node cpu01 (all runs; chosen at start because cpu05 was partly allocated to other users and cpu01 had the lowest allocation among cpu01-05; same Xeon Gold 5317 class), non-exclusive, one job at a time, `--qos=short` per run, per-seed arm order `Random(seed).shuffle(arms)` (plan `acc/ACC_PLAN_11jj.json`), no replacement or retry of any run, a run without summary.json is an apparatus failure kept in the inventory, chain stops after a failed smoke check, a refused submission or 3 consecutive runs without summary, job > 65 min cancelled. Outcome definitions and denominators as in METRIC_DEFINITIONS.md (receipt within the connector's 30 s limit; not sent; failed; unfinished; planned-without-record reported separately; chain-scan counts not used). Reported: service counters, u_t statistics, ai.status, survival split (process exit, OOM exception lines with time versus END, block-processing failure, block gaps), assert.txt FAIL lines per run (provisioning evidence: heap limit enforcement, flags). Statistics: same-seed paired differences, t 95 percent intervals over pair differences, per-pair values shown, no p-values, no ranking by interval overlap, no pooling across blocks.

**3. Blocks (arms as defined in 11ff/11ii: none; OracleBlock = ground-truth filter, non-deployable benchmark; PressureOnly = blanket shedding, ramp 0.60-0.90; SizeStatic = reject calldata > 65,604 B with p = 1; SizeGraded = same rule with p = grad(u_t); RandShed48 = content-blind random rejection p = 0.048; LearnedScore = Isolation Forest score with the frozen reference 0.5926 graded by u_t).**

Block TS (Besu G1 512 MB, attack sustained over 0-240 s = about 2,400 attack transactions, seeds 63401-63403): arms none, PressureOnly, SizeStatic, SizeGraded, SizeSticky; 15 runs.
Block TP (Besu G1 512 MB, attack pulse 60-90 s = about 300 attack transactions, seeds 63501-63503): same arms; 15 runs.
Block N (Nethermind build `~/diss_impl/nm/bin` nethermind.dll 4ad770c8c18a..., Nethermind.TxPool.dll 6923d71114a9..., 2048 MiB hex limit, attack burst, seeds 62101-62105): arms none, OracleBlock, PressureOnly, SizeStatic; 20 runs; post-run scan OOM exceptions are reported by time versus END as in 11hh.

Total 94 runs plus 5 smoke runs (one per mechanism: mixed counts attack 700-900 and look-alike 300-500 with all flagged rejected; ZGC LearnedScore service counters present; NM SizeStatic all flagged rejected; sustained attack >= 2,000 with sticky rejections; pulse 200-400 all flagged rejected), about 12.5 node-hours.

**4. Threshold disclosure.** 65,604 B was chosen after seeing 11ff-11hh results (largest benign training-class calldata; look-alike classes excluded from detector training by the 11q design); any threshold at or above 98,372 B would not flag the attack; the size rule is a tuned baseline.

**5. ## 11kk. Heavy-byte token-bucket admission ("Budget") against fixed-probability and static size rules: attack-intensity sweep, below-budget legitimate heavy stream, mixed traffic, ZGC and Nethermind; exploratory; registered 2026-11-19 (owner: build on the brainstorm; Slurm submissions delegated)

**1. Why.** The earlier campaigns show that a static calldata rule reproduces the ground-truth benchmark when the attack is size-separable, rejects an entire benign look-alike class otherwise, and that pressure-graded rules (driven by the lagging u_t) are weak. A brainstorm (Codex, Antigravity, Sonnet, Claude; 2026-11-19) converged on metering instead of rejecting and on a signal that does not lag. 11kk tests the simplest such policy, a heavy-byte token bucket, and states honestly that it is a class-selective token-bucket policer (prior art); a blind design review by Codex (2026-11-19) judged the contribution empirical, not algorithmic, and its essential points were adopted: three fixed budget values spanning a decade, the adaptive variant kept separate, logging of offered and admitted heavy bytes, a below-budget legitimate heavy stream test, and complete fixed-R intensity blocks first.

**2. ## 11mm. Per-sender heavy-byte metering and sender fragmentation (mechanistic characterization; exploratory); registered 2026-11-19 (owner: run it; Slurm submissions delegated; design reviewed blind by Codex 2026-11-19)

**1. Why.** 11kk showed that a global heavy-byte bucket cannot protect legitimate same-size heavy traffic when attack and legitimate heavy transactions share it (58-92% of look-alikes rejected, MX), and that the static size rule rejects all of them. 11mm characterizes the next simplest policy, one heavy-byte bucket per sender, and states plainly what it can show: attack and look-alike transactions are wire-identical, so the only information separating them here is the offered rate per sender account. The experiment therefore characterizes the behaviour of per-sender metering and its evasion mode (fragmentation over many accounts); it does NOT test whether metering detects attacks.
**2. Design.** Besu 25.1.0 r6, G1, 512 MB, 100 tx/s offered, 240 s, heavy window 60-180 s, one token per run (`besu:512m:100:g1:OFF:36:125000000:<seed>:C:new:`). Arms: none; SizeStatic (reject calldata > 65,604 B, p = 1); BudgetR250 (global bucket, 250 KB/s, capacity 200,000 B, as in 11kk); SenderBucket (one bucket per sender, 100 KB/s per sender, capacity 200,000 B, initial bucket full; the value was fixed before any data from the expected sender rates and is not tuned). n = 4 seeds per block (seeds 63811-14, 63821-24, 63831-34, 63841-44), 4 arms, 64 runs; per-seed shuffled arm order; smoke seeds 63899/63898/63897/63896 (SenderBucket, one per block).

## 11nn. Deferral instead of rejection: heavy-byte shaper vs policer (exploratory); registered 2026-11-19 (owner: "one more try"; Slurm submissions delegated; design reviewed blind by Codex 2026-11-19)

**1. Why.** 11kk and 11mm showed that class-blind or sender-rate-based admission cannot protect legitimate heavy transactions that look like the attack (wire-identical). A gate can still choose HOW it sheds: reject (11kk policer) or delay. 11nn asks only whether delaying, instead of rejecting, moves legitimate heavy traffic from "rejected" to "delivered late", and what that costs; it cannot separate attack from look-alike (the shaper is class-blind), and it is NOT expected to make the node healthier per se, because the deferred bytes still arrive, only later.
**2. Design.** Besu 25.1.0 r6, G1, 512 MB, 100 tx/s, heavy window 60-180 s; DUR = 360 s (not 240 s as in 11jj/11kk/11mm, to leave a drain period: deferred transactions can still be sent and confirmed before the harness stops; Caliper timeout DUR+90 s as before). Arms: none; SizeStatic (reject calldata > 65,604 B); BudgetR250 (11kk policer, 250 KB/s, capacity 200,000 B); ShapeR250D60 and ShapeR250D180 (heavy-byte shaper at 250 KB/s, burst allowance 200,000 B, FIFO virtual-time/GCRA; a heavy transaction that would have to wait longer than D = 60 s or 180 s is rejected; otherwise the gate answers `defer` with `delay_ms` and the load generator waits that long, then sends; small transactions pass at once). The policer equals the shaper with D = 0: unit test over 30 s with identical requests: policer 78 accepted, shaper (D = 0) 79, expected about 78. 60 runs; per-seed shuffled arm order; one block per node. One workload module for every arm and burst kind (`acc/nn/raacV5BurstDefer.js`, the 11jj mixed module plus deferral handling; it logs `defer_ms` and keeps the scheduled time, so the wire-send time is scheduled time + defer_ms).
**3. Outcomes.** For each of the attack and look-alike classes: terminal partition = sent at once (delay <= 20 ms) / deferred then sent / rejected (dropped: delay would exceed D) / unfinished at the end (right-censored); of the sent ones, confirmed within 30 s after sending AND completed (receipt) within 120 s of the scheduled time (end-to-end, the primary heavy-class metric); defer delay median and p90. For benign (non-heavy) transactions: receipts within the 30 s connector limit after sending and benign rejected, as before. Secondary: G1 pause total, peak RSS, max block gap. Same-seed paired differences vs none and vs the policer, t 95% CI, per-pair values, no p-values; labels and service counters are the basis; no run replaced; chain rules as in 11mm.
**4. Expected, stated before the data.** Deferral cannot separate classes: the FIFO share of look-alike equals its share of heavy traffic. In D1 and D2 (offered 0.98 MB/s) the shaper can release about 250 KB/s x (120 s + D) of heavy bytes; most of the rest is dropped at the delay cap. Delivered-late look-alike transactions replace some of the policer's rejected ones; unfinished or dropped fractions remain large. Node effect (benign receipts) is not expected to improve over the policer; it may worsen because delayed heavy transactions arrive while benign traffic continues. D3 (0.33 MB/s offered, just above R) is where deferral should clearly deliver legitimate heavy traffic that the policer rejects.
**5. Claim boundary.** Allowed: under this offered-load schedule and these parameters, global FIFO deferral reallocates indistinguishable heavy traffic from immediate rejection to late delivery, deadline drop or censored completion, with the stated latency cost. Not allowed: attack/look-alike separation; improved availability of legitimate heavy traffic without counting deadline drops, censoring and the delay; improved user-perceived service from post-send receipts alone; a general security or DoS result; node-pressure reduction beyond the measured GC/RSS/block-gap values; any parameter optimality.

Apparatus.** New files: `acc/acc_raac_run_11nn.sbatch` (sha256 ec07e2acd9be...), `acc/acc_selfcheck_11nn.py` (76def37067ad..., hardware class cpu01-10 as in 11mm; DUR 360 allowed per block), `acc/acc_raac_checked_11nn.sbatch` (089b5f8cbd30...), `acc/ACC_PINS_11nn.json`, `acc/ACC_SUBMIT_11nn.sh`, `acc/acc_chain_11nn.sh`, `acc/acc_finish_11nn.sh`, `acc/ACC_PLAN_11nn.json`, `acc/nn/raacV5BurstDefer.js` (26ea8b608fb2...), `acc/nn/shaper_service/` (serve_unified.py 8a472d846555..., copy of the 11mm service plus mode `shaper` and response field `delay_ms`; the 11kk budget service is used unchanged for BudgetR250), analysis/verification scripts. ## 11pp. History-weighted per-sender heavy-byte metering and the cost of buying history (exploratory); registered 2026-11-20 (owner: try one new metering technique; Slurm submissions delegated; design reviewed blind by Codex 2026-11-20)

**1. Why.** 11kk-11nn showed that class-blind metering (global bucket, flat per-sender bucket, deferral) cannot protect legitimate heavy transactions that are wire-identical to the attack, and that a flat per-sender bucket is bypassed by fragmentation. 11pp tests the one technique left that uses information a gate can observe itself: the history of the sender account. It does NOT claim to detect attacks or to be Sybil-resistant; it characterizes what history-weighted metering protects and how cheaply it can be defeated.
**2. Technique (fixed before the data, not tuned).** The gate counts, per sender account, the ordinary (non-heavy) transactions it has seen, h. Each sender has a heavy-byte token bucket (capacity 200,000 B, initial full, heavy = calldata > 65,604 B) whose refill rate is r(h) = r0 + (rmax - r0) x min(1, h / H) with r0 = 10 KB/s (fresh account), rmax = 400 KB/s (established), H = 100 ordinary transactions (about 30 s of a 3.33 tx/s sender); no decay of h. Sender id = load-generator worker account (gate reads it from the worker-index prefix of the local tx counter; a real gate would read the recovered sender address and build h from observed transactions). Service mode `hist_budget` (unit test over 12 s at 3.33 heavy tx/s: fresh sender 3 of 40 admitted, history 33 -> 18, history 100 -> 40; expected 3 / 19 / 51 before the cap effect).
**3. Design.** Besu 25.1.0 r6, G1, 512 MB, 100 tx/s, 240 s, heavy window 60-180 s, one token per run; heavy totals fixed per run (800-804 attack and 400-402 look-alike transactions, 98 KB). Attack accounts send nothing except their heavy transactions (and, where stated, an ordinary-traffic warm-up before the window), so "fresh" means h = 0. Blocks (a block is never split across nodes), attack accounts / look-alike accounts: Y1 2 fresh (3.33 heavy tx/s each) / 6 established (0.55 tx/s each); Y2 12 fresh (0.55 tx/s) / 6 established; Y3 12 accounts that send ordinary traffic for 10 s before the window (h = 33, adaptive attacker buying history) / 6 established; Y4 2 fresh / 1 established account sending heavy at 3.33 tx/s (327 KB/s, below rmax); Y5 2 accounts with a 60 s ordinary warm-up (h = 200 >= H, pre-aged) / 6 established; Y6 2 fresh / 6 FRESH look-alike accounts (false-positive cost: legitimate heavy senders without history). Established accounts send ordinary traffic throughout (h >= H by 30 s). Arms: none; SizeStatic; SenderBucket (flat per-sender 100 KB/s, as in 11mm); HistBucket. 4 seeds per block (64211-14, 64221-24, 64231-34, 64241-44, 64251-54, 64261-64; smoke seeds 64299 / 64297 / 64295 / 64294 for Y1 / Y3 / Y5 / Y6, HistBucket); 96 runs; per-seed shuffled arm order. Workload module `burstKind hist` (copy of the 11mm split module; role and silence rules; attack accounts and fresh look-alike accounts produce no transaction in silent slots, so planned-without-record counts are large and expected).
**4. Outcomes.** As in 11mm (look-alike and attack rejection per block and arm, benign receipts within 30 s, benign rejected, heavy admitted, survival); same-seed paired differences, t 95% CI, per-pair values, no p-values; labels and service counters are the basis; no run replaced; chain rules as in 11mm. Cost of buying history: the number of ordinary transactions an attacker account must send before the window (reported per block: 0 / 0 / 33 / 0 / 200 / 0).
**5. Expected, stated before the data (by construction).** Y1 and Y4: fresh attackers throttled to about r0, established look-alikes passed; Y2: 54 KB/s per fresh attacker versus r0 = 10 KB/s, mostly throttled; Y3: bypassed (33 ordinary transactions per account already give r(h) of about 139 KB/s > 54 KB/s, so a fragmented low-rate attack buys history cheaply); Y5: bypassed (pre-aged accounts at 400 KB/s exceed their 327 KB/s attack rate; the attack passes in full); Y6: look-alikes without history are throttled like the attack (false-positive cost). If the data differ, that is the finding.
**6. Claim boundary.** Allowed: under these fixed parameters and the assumed history distribution (account history = ordinary transactions seen by the gate; no stake, deposit or authentication), history-weighted metering protected established heavy senders against fresh low-history senders, while fragmented low-rate or pre-aged accounts bypassed it and fresh legitimate heavy senders were throttled. Not allowed: distinguishes spam from legitimate traffic; Sybil-resistant; attackers must incur meaningful cost (no cost model: gas, account acquisition, compromised accounts and history decay are not modelled and the pre-aging is 60 s, not days); protects against adaptive attackers; any claim about recovered sender addresses or production use. A sender-bound capability with stake or authentication (suggested by the reviewer of this design) is not tested.
**7. Apparatus.** New files: `acc/acc_raac_run_11pp.sbatch` (sha256 f9dade7d27ee..., copy of the 11mm launcher plus arm HistBucket, burst `hist`, warm-up/freshness injection), `acc/acc_selfcheck_11pp.py` (811b9f1ad477...), `acc/acc_raac_checked_11pp.sbatch` (94b4958b94b9...), `acc/ACC_PINS_11pp.json`, `acc/ACC_SUBMIT_11pp.sh`, `acc/acc_chain_11pp.sh`, `acc/acc_finish_11pp.sh`, `acc/ACC_PLAN_11pp.json`, `acc/pp/raacV5BurstHist.js` (53175dab4887...), `acc/pp/hist_service/` (serve_unified.py 281e8888c9dc..., copy of the 11mm service plus mode `hist_budget`; the 11mm sender service is used unchanged for SenderBucket), `acc11pp_analysis.py`, `verify_11pp.py`. Hardware class cpu01-10 as in 11mm.

Amendment during the first runs, before any analysis (2026-11-20 ~08:15, after a blind review by Codex).** About 20 registered runs of the four-arm design had started when this amendment was made (runs are kept; no result had been read). (a) Two flat-budget controls are added to every block so that the effect of history can be separated from the effect of the budget values: SenderLow10 (flat per-sender bucket at 10 KB/s for every account, the fresh-account rate) and SenderHigh400 (flat 400 KB/s for every account, the established-account rate); capacity 200,000 B as before; same service as SenderBucket. Gate-observed submissions are easier to forge than on-chain history, so the bypass cost measured here is a lower bound for an on-chain-history gate. (c) Aggregate allowed heavy rate (sum over the heavy-sending accounts at the start of the window; offered heavy rate in every run is about 984 KB/s = 656 attack + 328 look-alike): Y1 (2 attack + 6 look-alike accounts): flat 100 -> 800 KB/s, flat 10 -> 80, flat 400 -> 3,200, history -> 2 x 10 + 6 x 400 = 2,420; Y2 (12 + 6): 1,800 / 180 / 7,200 / 12 x 10 + 2,400 = 2,520; Y3 (12 warmed to h = 33, r about 139 KB/s, + 6): 1,800 / 180 / 7,200 / about 4,070; Y4 (2 + 1): 300 / 30 / 1,200 / 2 x 10 + 400 = 420; Y5 (2 pre-aged to h = 200 + 6): 800 / 80 / 3,200 / 2 x 400 + 2,400 = 3,200; Y6 (2 fresh + 6 fresh look-alike): 800 / 80 / 3,200 / 8 x 10 = 80. The report shows this table next to the results because the total budget grows with the number of accounts. (e) Block Y2-vs-Y1 comparisons are not made (benign background load differs by block because silent attack accounts send no ordinary traffic); arms are compared within a block.
